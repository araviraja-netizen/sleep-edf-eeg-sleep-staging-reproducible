## Retrieval limits and citation integrity

Eight of the ten original Crossref refresh requests succeeded with matching titles. The DeepSleepNet and Kemp EEG-study requests returned HTTP 429 (rate limiting); their preserved original metadata remain available, and DeepSleepNet's author manuscript was accessible. Both newly added references were retrieved successfully through Crossref. These access outcomes do not change article identity or justify invented metadata.

The PhysioNet dataset landing page timed out in this session; its previously verified record and DOI are retained. The PMC page initially presented a browser challenge; the original article's indexed abstract and published-paper search record support the qualitative R11 claim. A legacy AASM PDF URL returned 404 and is not used as the manuscript's canonical link. Publisher DOI redirects and OpenReview may apply access controls; resolving bibliographic identity through publisher-deposited metadata and author/official records does not imply that every publisher page was accessible. The local verification report distinguishes local link resolution from live external retrieval.

No reference has been invented. The original package's 13 references are retained and two verified primary references are added. R11 remains DOI **10.5664/jcsm.2350**, pages 81–87; DOI 2352 belongs to a different commentary and is not substituted. No reference supports a state-of-the-art, first-ever, clinical-effectiveness or causal-mechanism claim for this study. Related work now explicitly covers representations/context, minority-class objectives, subject-independent versus cross-cohort evaluation, and efficiency. Claims of broad novelty would require a separately designed literature review and stronger experimental evidence.
