**Background:** Compact single-channel EEG models offer a practical basis for automatic sleep staging, but model comparisons depend on subject allocation, temporal context and evaluation procedures. **Objective:** Compare a CNN, a CNN–BiLSTM and a compact CNN–Transformer under a preserved subject-independent protocol, with separate estimates of training-seed variability and test-subject uncertainty. **Methods:** Sleep Cassette recordings from {{SC_subjects}} subjects were allocated to {{train_subjects}} training, {{validation_subjects}} validation and {{test_subjects}} test subjects. Fpz-Cz EEG was filtered continuously and segmented into 30-second epochs. Annotation-based 30-minute Wake margins yielded {{protocol_A_epochs}} eligible epochs. Best-validation checkpoints were fixed before final evaluation on {{test_epochs}} test epochs. The CNN had one completed seed; both context models had three predefined seeds. Paired subject bootstrap intervals used {{bootstrap_draws}} resamples. **Results:** Test macro-F1 was {{test_M1_macro_f1}} for the CNN, {{test_M5_macro_f1}} ± {{test_M5_macro_f1_sd}} for CNN–BiLSTM and {{test_M7_macro_f1}} ± {{test_M7_macro_f1_sd}} for CNN–Transformer; ± denotes training-seed sample SD. Accuracy was {{test_M1_accuracy_percent}}%, {{test_M5_accuracy_percent}}% and {{test_M7_accuracy_percent}}%, respectively. The Transformer-minus-BiLSTM macro-F1 difference was {{M7_minus_M5_delta}}, with a 95% subject-paired interval [{{M7_minus_M5_low}}, {{M7_minus_M5_high}}], which does not establish reliable superiority. N1 remained challenging, and context-model transition performance did not mirror overall gains. **Conclusion:** The study provides an auditable comparison of compact established model families. Unequal seed counts, context differences and a small single-cohort holdout limit architectural and generalization claims. The test set is now consumed; further model development requires independent evaluation data.
